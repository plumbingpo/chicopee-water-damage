Insurance and Water Damage: A Guide for Boston Owners

A claim is decided on what can be shown, which makes documentation the most useful tool a Boston owner has. Start building it before anything is cleaned up or thrown away.

Photograph the source of the water, such as the split hose or the failed fitting, and keep the broken part if you can. Shoot every room from the doorway, then move in for close-ups of ruined belongings and the model numbers on electrical goods. Hold damaged flooring and possessions until your adjuster has inspected them or said that pictures are enough. Keep receipts for anything you spend because of the loss, from fans to a hotel stay.

Insurers often draw a line between sudden damage, like a burst pipe, and damage from a slow leak or outside flooding. Water Damage We See Most in Boston

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Boston and you will hear the same six answers. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in Boston. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Boston homes is well along before a stain appears. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Chicopee Restoration crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.
